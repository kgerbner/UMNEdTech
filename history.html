The history of educational technology at Minnesota

Minnesota has an outsized place in the history of educational computing: the state that gave the world The Oregon Trail is also home to the university whose campus mascot lent its name to the Gopher protocol — for a moment in the early 1990s, the front door to the internet. This timeline traces how teaching technology arrived, changed, and was contested at the University of Minnesota. Every event cites its sources.

Hear it firsthand: the Charles Babbage Institute — the world's leading computing-history archive, housed at UMN — holds oral histories with the people in this timeline, with transcripts free online: Marvin Stein ↗ (hired in 1955 to teach UMN's first computer courses), Dale LaFrenz ↗ (MECC co-founder, from Dartmouth BASIC to The Oregon Trail), Peter Patton ↗ (UMN academic computing director, on PLATO courses in the humanities), William Norris ↗ (ERA and Control Data), and Mark McCahill ↗ (the Gopher protocol, from inside UMN computing services). Browse the full collection at CBI Oral Histories ↗, and see digitized photographs and documents — from the 1958 dedication of UMN's first computer ↗ to an original "Internet Gopher" services pamphlet ↗ — in UMedia ↗, the Libraries' digital collections.
