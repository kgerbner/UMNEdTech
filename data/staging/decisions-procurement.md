# Staging: Who makes ed tech contract decisions at UMN (research notes, June 2026)

## 1. Procurement chain (VERIFIED unless noted)

- Purchasing Services oversees all UMN purchasing (sourcing, bidding); sits in the Controller's Office, part of University Finance. (purchasing.umn.edu; controller.umn.edu/units) [Names Beth Tapp (dir.), Mollie Viola (AVP & Controller): single-source, do not publish]
- Thresholds (policy.umn.edu/finance/purchasing + /finance/purchasing-appa + purchasing.umn.edu/policy-and-process):
  - $0–$9,999: department picks the supplier
  - $10,000–$49,999: department files a "Price and Supplier Justification" form
  - $50,000+: competitive process (RFx) run by Purchasing Services, or a written "Request for Exception" (sole source/single source) approved by Purchasing before ordering. Splitting purchases to evade bidding is prohibited.
  - $250,000+ even from U-Wide contract suppliers → competitive process
- Board of Regents: $5,000,000+ purchases must be presented to the Board for approval (after bidding, before signing); value includes optional extensions; amendments increasing a Board-approved deal 30%+ also go to the Board. $1,000,000+ purchases are REPORTED in the Finance & Operations Committee consent report ("Purchase of Goods and Services $1,000,000 and Over"). (policy.umn.edu/finance/purchasing; regents.umn.edu Reservation and Delegation of Authority PDF 2024-10; F&O dockets)
- OGC: all contracts must use OGC standard contract templates or be reviewed/approved by OGC (Contract Review Form, ~3 weeks); for $50k+ consult Purchasing first. (ogc.umn.edu/contracts; policy.umn.edu/operations/contracts)
- Signing: only employees with WRITTEN delegated authority may sign; chain = Regents Policy: Reservation and Delegation of Authority (2001, current 2024) → President → sub-delegations (policy.umn.edu/operations/delegations). Searchable Delegations of Authority Library (authority.umn.edu). Unauthorized signature = no University obligation + personal liability.
- Software-specific: "Process for UMN Software Purchases" (it.umn.edu self-help guide): Vendor Risk Assessment (VRA) by University Information Security (security@umn.edu), HIPCO/Controller/ASR compliance partners, simultaneous Digital Accessibility in Procurement procedure; OGC review may be needed even for free tools. Atlas software portfolio check. U Market = primary purchasing tool.

## 2. OIT / technology decision-makers (VERIFIED unless noted)

- VP for IT & CIO: Tarek Tomes since March 16, 2026 (came from State of Minnesota CIO role; reports to EVP Finance & Operations Gregg Goldman). Bernie Gulachek (CIO since 2015) retired Aug 15, 2025; CISO Brian Dahlin served as interim. (brief.umn.edu announcements; MeriTalk)
- OIT operates the systemwide academic stack (Canvas, Kaltura, Zoom, Honorlock — Honorlock centrally funded, selected "after a rigorous RFP process" per it.umn.edu). Academic Technology Support Services (ATSS) is OIT's instructor-facing team; Duluth has parallel ITSS.
- New-software gatekeeping: University Information Security runs a Vendor Risk Assessment (VRA, ~3–6 weeks, security@umn.edu) coordinating with HIPCO/Controller/ASR; Digital Accessibility in Procurement runs in parallel; buyers directed to check the Atlas Technology Portfolio for existing solutions ("requirement" strength unverified). (it.umn.edu Process for UMN Software Purchases; policy.umn.edu/it/securedata-appw)
- Governance: OIT runs an annual systemwide IT governance input cycle feeding budget priorities (governance.it.umn.edu). The Senate Committee on Information Technologies (SCIT) — 8 faculty, 4 academic professionals, 3 students, 1 civil service — advises the president/VPs/CIO, reviews major IT deployments; active through at least Sept 2025 (usenate.umn.edu; minutes in Digital Conservancy).
- HECVAT usage by UMN: UNVERIFIED. Named security review preceding Honorlock: not found beyond the RFP.

## 5. Academic voice / how decisions actually played out (VERIFIED unless noted)

- LMS choice (2015–17): University Learning Technology Advisors (ULTA) — faculty/staff committee, 28 faculty representing each college and campus, chaired by Prof. Lee-Ann Breuch — ran the Canvas-vs-Moodle evaluation; fall 2016 pilot survey: ~80% of instructors and ~60% of students favored Canvas; open forums + vendor demos March 2017; ULTA formally recommended April 2017; FINAL DECISION by EVP/Provost Karen Hanson and VP-CIO Bernard Gulachek, announced June 7, 2017. (ulta.umn.edu; mndaily; provost LMS review condensed report PDF)
- Earlier: May 2009 "Future CMS" workgroup chose Moodle after Blackboard killed WebVista (leadership/membership unconfirmed).
- Proctoring: spring 2021 Minnesota Student Association resolution on Proctorio (privacy/equity) → routed through Senate committees → Provost's "Digital Privacy and E-Proctoring Task Force" convened spring 2022 at SCEP's request (chair Senenge Andzenge — single source); recommendations on proctoring practices and student rights presented to SCIT and SCEP fall 2023 (single source). 2024 Honorlock RFP: evaluation committee composition NOT public — "the academic technology community" tested the tool (single source). TRANSPARENCY GAP worth stating on site.
- Senate bodies: SCIT (represents faculty/staff/student interests in IT, advises president/VPs/CIO); SCEP (educational policy, requested the proctoring task force, owns GenAI syllabus statements); SAIC (academic integrity, discussed Proctorio 2021, task force update 2023).
